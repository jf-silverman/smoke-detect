# About the Project Author

Hello! I'm Joel Silverman, a data scientist with an unusual background.

I started my career as a wildland firefighter and biologist for the National Park Service and the
Forest Service, working in New Mexico, Washington, Oregon, and Idaho. Not only did I get to fight
wildfires and manage prescribed fires, I also collected and analyzed data on the effects of fire on
ecosystems.

After a few years, I decided to go back to school for a Master of Science degree. I applied for and
won a fully funded graduate research assistantship at Colorado State University, sponsored by NASA. The
position aimed to help land managers make better use of satellite-derived imagery to answer questions
about invasive plants and wildfires. I got to work with both amazing NASA scientists and renowned land
managers at Sequoia–Kings Canyon, Grand Teton, and Yellowstone National Parks, as well as staff at the
Washington Office. I ended up using specialized burned-area imagery (dNBR) to predict where invasive,
non-native plants would take hold, producing maps for early-detection crews.

After grad school, I took a position as the Lead Fire Effects Monitor at Zion National Park, and later
a temporary promotion as Zion's Fire Ecologist. I worked on the fireline with different crews and
helped plan and analyze prescribed fires. From there I took a natural resources position with a
prescribed burn program, but the program was put on hold pending an update to the CAL FIRE prescribed
burn program. I moved on to a job with the Forest Service in the Sierra, where my primary duty was
managing several wilderness areas — though it also included summer and fall work on wildfires, often in
wilderness. I worked on several large-scale fires that had high impacts on surrounding communities,
frequently serving as a Resource Advisor and sometimes as a Geographic Information Systems (GIS)
specialist.

Like many people, my life changed drastically during COVID. My immediate family moved to the Bay Area
to be closer to relatives and for job opportunities. I took a full-time, three-month intensive Python
coding and data science bootcamp to build on the data-modeling foundation from grad school. Since then,
I've worked at a medical device company in R&D, where I still analyze images — albeit in a completely
different context. I started as a data analyst and have since been promoted to staff data scientist. I
still have an active interest in wildland fire, and I hope to better understand both object detection
and smoke detection through this personal project.
